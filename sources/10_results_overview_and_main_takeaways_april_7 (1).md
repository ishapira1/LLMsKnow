# Consolidated summary of results

Data sources:
- Friction analysis: 4 paired runs, clean test subset, 6,379 items total, 5,065 neutral-correct items.
- Claim-3 analysis: selected-layer wide bundle with 130,264 model rows and 521,336 probe rows across 4 runs and 5 framing families.

Main conclusions:
1. Behavioral sycophancy is real and targeted toward the user-backed wrong answer.
2. Friction is strongly supported as a movement phenomenon, not merely a flip phenomenon.
3. Neutral hidden knowledge exists, but is partial and most visible on model-error items.
4. The same neutral readout usually does not survive biased framings cleanly.
5. Truth remains strongly decodable within framing, especially with matched-family probes.
6. The best interpretation is representation shift or re-encoding, not full erasure and not broad stable override.
7. A small override-like slice exists, but it is not the dominant regime.
8. Claim 3, strong framing-agnostic truth detection, is not established by the current experiments.
